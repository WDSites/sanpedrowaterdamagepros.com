Crawl Space Water Damage Cleanup in San Pedro, CA

Water under a house is hard to see and easy to ignore. We find it, remove it and dry the floor framing above before problems reach the rooms.

A crawl space is the shallow area between the ground and the floor of a raised house. It holds plumbing, ducts and floor framing, and when water gets in it can soak insulation, rot wood and push damp air into the rooms above.

This page explains how crawl space water damage happens, what we do about it and what the building code expects. It also covers the honest question of how common it is here.

How common is this in San Pedro?

Many older homes in the area sit on raised foundations with crawl spaces, while many newer homes are built slab on grade. If your house sits on a slab, this page does not apply and the water damage cleanup page is a better fit. If you can see a small access hatch in a closet floor or a vent along the foundation, read on.

Winter rain matters here, since about 14 inches a year fall in San Pedro and most arrives between January and March. A leaking pipe can also fill the space slowly, long before any room shows a stain.

Crawl space safety before anyone goes in

Do not crawl into a wet space yourself. Wiring, ducts and pipes run through it, the space is tight and standing water can hide electrical hazards. Shut off power to any circuit near wet areas from the panel, and shut off the main water valve if you suspect a leak.

Keep children and pets away from open access hatches. If sewage or a strong odor is present, treat it as contaminated water and stay out. Our crews use protective gear and lighting, and they work to the ANSI/IICRC S500 reference standard for water damage.

What the California Residential Code expects

The California Residential Code, the state building code for houses, sets rules for under-floor spaces. Vented crawl spaces need at least 1 square foot of net opening for every 150 square feet of under-floor area. That can be reduced to 1/1,500 when the ground is covered with an approved Class I vapor retarder.

Unvented crawl spaces are meant to have a continuous ground cover, with joints lapped 6 inches and sealed, and edges running at least 6 inches up the stem wall. A floor access opening must be at least 18 by 24 inches. If groundwater can rise within 6 inches of the finished floor at the perimeter, the grade must be raised or a drainage system installed.

Local notes for crawl space owners

The Los Angeles Department of Water and Power supplies water across the city, and its meter sits outside the house. The pipe from that meter to your home belongs to you, so a leak under the floor on that line is yours to repair, and a plumber can test it.

For rebuild work such as new subfloor or framing, ask the Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety whether the work needs a permit. In Rancho Palos Verdes and parts of the hillsides, slope and drainage conditions also matter, so ask a contractor about water paths around the foundation.

Insurance and prevention

Insurers often distinguish sudden pipe failures from slow seepage and from water that rises from the ground. Sudden plumbing leaks may be covered. Groundwater and long term moisture commonly are not. Ask your insurer which category applies, and keep photos of the hatch before and after cleanup.

Prevention is simple. Keep vents clear, direct downspouts away from the foundation, repair pipe leaks promptly and ask a contractor about a vapor barrier. Never block vents with soil or planters. Questions about crawl space water damage

Is emergency crawl space cleanup available in San Pedro?

Yes. Phone whenever you notice it. If you suspect a pipe, close the main valve first, and we will plan how to get lights, pumps and drying gear through your access opening.

Does my house even have a crawl space?

Look for a small hatch in a closet floor or a vent along the foundation. Slab houses do not have one.

Can I just run a dehumidifier under the house?

Not safely on your own. The water and the wiring need to be checked first, and a crew can place equipment correctly.

Will wet insulation need to be replaced?

Usually yes. It holds water against the floor and loses its value once soaked.

Is crawl space water covered by insurance?

A sudden plumbing leak may be. Groundwater and gradual seepage often are not. Ask your insurer.
