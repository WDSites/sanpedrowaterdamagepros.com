A big city, several water systems

Torrance had 147,067 residents at the 2020 census and was incorporated on May 12, 1921, after a planned community was laid out by developer Jared Sidney Torrance with designs from Frederick Law Olmsted Jr. Its size matters for a water call, because different parts of the city get water from different systems.

Torrance Municipal Water serves about 105,000 residents and business customers across roughly 78 percent of the city. California Water Service serves other parts through its Dominguez and Hermosa-Redondo systems. Your bill tells you which one is yours.

When water is spreading in Torrance

Typical emergency calls start with water heaters, dishwashers, washing machine hoses and supply lines in homes of many ages. The emergency line is open 24/7. Close the fixture valve, or the main, lift belongings and stay away from wet outlets.

Some western Torrance addresses carry a Redondo Beach mailing address, so give your street name when you call rather than only the city on your mail.

After-hours water emergencies in the city system

The municipal water department says its crews respond around the clock to emergencies and outages, and water main breaks after business hours go through the city's fire dispatch line. If water is running in a street or from a city meter, report that to the city. For water inside your building, call us.

Torrance has about 1.5 miles of beachfront on Santa Monica Bay and a large industrial base that includes a refinery, American Honda's U.S. headquarters and helicopter manufacturing. Larger commercial buildings need a written drying plan that counts equipment.

Old Torrance, Madrona Marsh and the Del Amo area

Old Torrance was laid out on a diagonal to channel ocean breezes, and the El Prado Bridge dates to 1913. Madrona Marsh, an urban wetland on former oil production land, and the Del Amo Fashion Center, among the ten largest malls in the country by leasable area, are familiar reference points. Naming one helps dispatch place a call.

About 54 percent of occupied Torrance homes are owner-occupied, which means many calls turn into insurance claims. Keep photos, receipts and written scope documents together, and ask your insurer about your policy.

Before we arrive: if water is still spreading

Stop the supply

Close the fixture valve, then the main valve. Know where your main is before an emergency.

Power safety

Turn off power to wet rooms only from dry ground. If water touches a panel or outlet, call 911 or the utility.

Protect the contents

Lift furniture, rugs and electronics. Put foil under furniture legs on wet carpet.

Stay out of contaminated water

Sewage or backed-up drain water needs protective handling. Keep children and pets out.
