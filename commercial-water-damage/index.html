A commercial water loss is different from a home loss in one important way. More people have a say. There is an owner, perhaps a property manager, one or more tenants, and sometimes a landlord's insurer and a tenant's insurer. This page explains what our crews can do and how that work fits around everyone else.

We keep the promise narrow on purpose. Our crews handle emergency water response, extraction and drying, plus documentation. Plumbing repair, construction and insurance decisions belong to other people.

Where San Pedro businesses meet water trouble

By its own description, the Port of Los Angeles lies in San Pedro Bay about 25 miles from downtown Los Angeles. It covers 7,500 acres of land and water across 43 miles of waterfront and has ranked as the nation's top container port every year since 2000.

A working waterfront means many buildings of many ages, from older storefronts to newer warehouses and offices, and each handles water differently.

The causes are familiar. A failed supply line above a ceiling, a leaking roof during winter rain, a clogged drain or a broken water heater can all put water into a unit. Roughly 14 inches of rain falls in a year, mostly January through March, which is when roof and drain problems tend to surface.

Safety first in a shared building

  1. Get people out of wet areas

    Keep staff and customers away from wet floors and sagging ceilings.

  2. Find the shut-off

    The property manager or building engineer usually knows where the water shut-off is. Close it if it is safe.

  3. Think about power

    Do not touch wet electrical equipment. Shut off power to the area from a dry location if you can do so safely.

  4. Document and call

    Take photos of the damage and call the 24/7 line with the address and what is wet.

Tell the property manager as soon as you can. A leak that starts in one unit may be spreading into the one below it.

What the crew can do on site

The crew extracts standing water with pumps and wet vacuums, then uses moisture meters and a thermal camera to find the water that has soaked into drywall, ceilings, insulation and flooring. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the space, and wet material that cannot be saved is removed. Methods are drawn from ANSI/IICRC S500, the published trade reference, and it implies no certification.

The crew also records what it finds. Photos, moisture readings and a drying log help the owner, the tenant and their insurers understand what happened. The emergency water removal page explains the first stage in more detail, and the structural drying page covers the drying methods.

Working around tenants, managers and access

Commercial work often needs coordination. Someone has to give access to the space, say which areas are off limits and tell the crew where shut-offs, panels and ceilings are located. Our crews will work with the property manager and the tenant, and the 24/7 line can handle after-hours calls when a loss happens overnight or on a weekend.

A few questions come up often. Who can authorize the work? Who holds the keys? Can equipment stay in the space overnight? Having those answers ready speeds up the first visit.

Local note: permits and who pays

Before walls or ceiling systems are rebuilt, check with the Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety on whether a permit applies. In many commercial leases, the landlord is responsible for the structure and the tenant for its own contents and improvements. Lease wording varies, so read yours or ask a professional.

The 2020 census counted 147,067 residents in Torrance, and businesses across Torrance and Harbor City can call the same line. Business insurance and any business interruption cover are questions for your insurer, who alone decides what is covered.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Waiting for a full plan before calling about active water.
  • Leaving wet ceiling tile or insulation in place.
  • Not telling neighboring tenants when a leak may spread.
  • Forgetting to photograph damage before anything is moved.

Questions about commercial water damage

Is emergency commercial water damage service available in San Pedro?

Yes. Give the address, what is wet and who holds the shut-off. The first step is usually extraction and a moisture survey, with the manager's approval for access.

Who authorizes the work, the owner or the tenant?

It depends on the lease and the situation. Have the property manager or owner confirm who can approve work and who can give access.

Can you work after business hours?

The 24/7 line takes calls at any hour, and crews are sent when available. Overnight access should be arranged with the property manager.

Do you help with insurance paperwork?

We provide photos, readings and drying logs. Coverage decisions are made by the insurer.

Does the work follow an industry standard?

Crews draw on ANSI/IICRC S500 as a trade reference and make no further claim.
