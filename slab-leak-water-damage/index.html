A slab leak sends water up through a concrete floor into carpet, baseboards and walls. The plumber fixes the line, and our crews dry and restore what it soaked.

Many Southern California homes sit on a concrete slab instead of a raised floor, and the water lines often run in or under that concrete. When one of those lines fails, water has nowhere to go but up. It finds the seams in the slab, then wicks into whatever lies on top of it.

Slab leaks are not the most common cause of water damage in San Pedro, but they are common enough in slab homes that every crew sees them.

This page covers the damage side of the problem. Finding and repairing the pipe is a plumber's job, and we will say so plainly. Once the water is off, our crews take over to dry the structure and clean what got wet.

Signs the water is coming from under the floor

A slab leak rarely announces itself with a gusher. It shows up as small, odd clues that add up. Because the water is hidden, a leak can run for weeks before a floor finally gives it away.

First hour safety steps

  1. Turn off the main

    Close the main shut-off valve for the house. If you are unsure where it is, find it before an emergency happens.

  2. Check the meter

    With every fixture off, watch the water meter. If it keeps moving, the leak is still active.

  3. Mind the power

    If water is near outlets, power strips or appliances, stay off wet floors and shut off power to that area from a dry spot.

  4. Call the plumber and the line

    A plumber locates and repairs the line. The 24/7 line sends a crew to handle the drying.

Move rugs, shoes and electronics off the wet area. Take photos of every wet surface before anything is moved, since those pictures help with a claim later.

Where the line runs and who fixes what

Water reaches a home through a public main in the street, then a private line to the house. In the city of Los Angeles the Los Angeles Department of Water and Power serves San Pedro. In August 2022 a suspected main break sent water across North Avalon Boulevard in Wilmington until LADWP crews could shut it off. A failure like that sits on the public side.

A leak under your own slab sits on your side of the meter.

That difference matters for who repairs the pipe. For the private line, a plumber uses acoustic listening gear, pressure tests and sometimes thermal imaging to pinpoint the leak. Repairs range from spot repair through the slab to rerouting the line through a wall or attic.

We stay out of that decision, but we coordinate timing so drying equipment is not in the plumber's way.

How we dry a floor that is wet from below

Water under concrete drains slowly, so the slab itself becomes a reservoir. The crew starts with moisture meters and a thermal camera to map the wet zone. Wet carpet pad and soaked baseboards usually come out, and the carpet may be lifted and dried or replaced depending on the water type and how long it sat.

Air movers push dry air across the surface, and dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the room.

Daily readings on the slab and nearby walls show when the drying is done. Crews work from the S500 guidance published by ANSI and the IICRC, a published trade reference and not a credential. The water mitigation page explains the larger emergency process, and burst supply lines are covered separately under burst pipe water damage.

Local note: ground movement and old lines

Land in this part of Los Angeles County does move. In September 2024 the governor declared an emergency over land movement on the Palos Verdes Peninsula, and hundreds of homes lost utility service.

We make no claim that ground movement caused any particular leak. As a general plumbing point, shifting soil can stress buried lines, which is one more reason to check your meter if a floor starts to feel damp.

Crews also cover homes in Rancho Palos Verdes and Wilmington, where the age of the plumbing and the type of foundation vary block by block.

Does insurance see a slab leak as sudden?

Policies often treat a sudden, accidental discharge differently from a long, slow leak. A leak under a slab can be hard to date, so the wording of your policy matters. Some policies also handle the cost of reaching the pipe differently from the cost of the water damage. Ask your insurer how your policy reads. Hold on to the plumber's report, your photos and the drying log, since all three tell the story.

Mistakes that make slab leaks worse

  • Waiting to see whether the damp spot goes away by itself.
  • Covering a wet floor with a rug or new flooring before the slab is dry.
  • Running a fan in the room without a plan to remove the moisture from the air.
  • Breaking into the slab yourself and hitting another line.

Questions about slab leak cleanup

Is emergency slab leak cleanup available in San Pedro?

Yes. Shut the main valve, then call. The crew starts with a meter and thermal camera sweep of the wet slab area while the plumber works on the pipe.

Do you find and fix the leak in the pipe?

No. A plumber finds and repairs the line. We dry the structure and restore what the water damaged.

Can the slab itself be dried?

Yes, in most cases. Concrete holds water for a long time, so readings are taken until the slab and the materials on top of it reach dry levels.

Will the carpet have to be replaced?

Not always. Clean water caught early may allow carpet to be lifted and dried, while pad usually comes out. A crew checks it on site.

Are slab foundations common in San Pedro?

They are common enough in newer construction, but many older homes have raised floors instead. A raised floor is a crawl space, which is covered on its own page.
