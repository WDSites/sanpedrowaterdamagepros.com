What a street flood on Avalon Boulevard shows

On a Friday in August 2022, water flooded the 400 block of North Avalon Boulevard in Wilmington while LADWP crews worked to shut the flow off. A suspected water main break was the working explanation, and the water was off by late afternoon. A construction site for affordable housing was reported as a total loss, and nobody was injured.

The practical lesson is who owns which problem. Water in the street belongs to the utility, and water that reaches a building belongs on your list. If you see water coming up in the street or at the meter, call LADWP about the main. If it is inside, close your valve and call us for extraction and drying.

Most Wilmington homes are rented, and that changes who calls

Census-era figures show that about six in ten Wilmington homes are rented, and the median age is 28. In practice that means a leak often belongs to a landlord, and the tenant is the one standing in the water. Tenants should photograph the damage, tell the owner or manager in writing the same day and ask who authorizes the work.

The crew can document moisture and write a scope for either party. Delays hurt everyone, because water that sits for more than a day or two starts to damage materials and create mold risk. If you are in an apartment, tell the units below you, since leaks follow gravity.

Port-area and industrial buildings

Wilmington sits beside the Port of Los Angeles and carries heavy industry. Oil refineries cover nearly a fifth of the land area, and the Wilmington Oil Field was discovered in 1932. Commercial, warehouse and shop buildings in this setting tend to be larger than homes, which means more floor area to dry and a written drying plan that counts equipment.

Buildings toward Terminal Island, reached by State Route 47 and the Vincent Thomas Bridge, get the same assessment. The plan names the areas, the equipment and the readings so a property manager or insurer can follow it.

Before we arrive: if water is still spreading in Wilmington

Close the valve

Turn off the fixture valve or the main valve at the house. If the main valve will not turn, ask LADWP how to shut off water at the meter.

Mind the power

Switch off power to wet rooms only if you can reach the panel on dry ground. If water touches outlets or a panel, stay out and call 911 or the utility.

Lift and photograph

Move small items up, put foil under furniture legs on wet carpet and take photos before anything is thrown away.

Keep everyone out of sewage

Water from a drain or toilet is contaminated. Keep children and pets away and do not run fans.
