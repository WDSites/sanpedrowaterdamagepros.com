What a burst pipe does to a home

A burst pipe is a pressurized line that has opened up. Household supply lines run at pressure all day, so a split keeps pushing water out until someone closes a valve. A small crack can sit hidden inside a wall for days. A full break can put a floor under water in minutes.

In San Pedro the usual causes are age and corrosion, a failed fitting or a hose connection that let go. Freezing is not a normal cause on this coastal peninsula, so a burst here almost always points to a worn part, a bad repair or a pressure surge.

Shutoff and safety in the first hour

  1. Close the water

    Turn the main shutoff on your house clockwise until it stops. If you cannot find it or it will not turn, say so when you call.

  2. Think about the power

    Water and electricity do not mix. If water is reaching outlets, a panel or a lit fixture, leave the breaker alone unless you can reach it from dry ground, and call an electrician or the line.

  3. Open the taps

    Run a faucet at the lowest point of the house to drain pressure from the pipes once the main is closed.

  4. Move what you can

    Lift small furniture, rugs and electronics out of the wet area and photograph everything before you move it.

  5. Call the 24/7 line

    Describe the room, how long the water ran and whether it touched a ceiling below. A crew can then plan the pump-out.

Your pipe or the city main?

Not every water problem at your address is yours. During a flooding event in August 2022, LADWP crews were closing valves on North Avalon Boulevard in Wilmington. A failure like that sits in a public main, and the utility handles it. A split on the house side of your meter is your own line and your own cleanup.

The Los Angeles Department of Water and Power supplies San Pedro with water. If you are not sure which side of the meter the leak is on, tell the line what you see and call the utility too.

Renting changes who acts first. Renters are the majority in Wilmington, where roughly 60 percent of homes are rented in Census-era figures, and in Harbor City, where the Census puts rentals a bit above half. Tenants should shut the water off, photograph the damage and notify the landlord in writing the same day.

How the cleanup runs

The crew starts by confirming the water is off and finding every wet surface. Moisture meters read drywall, baseboard and subfloor, and a thermal camera shows cool, wet areas behind finished walls. Standing water comes out first with submersible pumps and extraction wands, as described under emergency water removal.

Next comes removal of what cannot dry. Soaked insulation, saturated carpet pad and swollen baseboard are cut out, and drywall is opened where a line ran inside the wall. Air movers and a low-grain dehumidifier then dry the rest while readings are logged each visit. Technicians commonly check a drying plan against ANSI/IICRC S500, the published water damage reference.

Drying and monitoring are covered in more detail under water mitigation. Once readings are dry, rebuilding is a separate step, and the repair page explains it.

Insurance and burst pipes

Many homeowner policies treat a sudden, accidental burst differently from a slow leak that went unnoticed for weeks. Sudden events are often covered in part, and gradual damage is often excluded. The wording varies by policy, so read yours and call the insurer early.

Keep the damaged materials until the adjuster has seen them if you can, take photos before cleanup and save every receipt. The pipe repair itself is usually a plumber's invoice, separate from the drying and rebuild.

Mistakes that make it worse

  • Waiting for the plumber before closing the main.
  • Using a household vacuum on standing water.
  • Leaving wet drywall closed because the surface looks dry.
  • Running a space heater to dry a wall, which can bake the surface and leave the inside wet.
  • Putting off the call because the water looks clean.

Questions about burst pipe cleanup

Do you handle a burst pipe emergency overnight?

Yes, the line is staffed around the clock. The first question is whether the main is closed. Once it is, the crew plans pumps, a moisture survey and wall openings based on what you describe.

Should I call a plumber or a cleanup crew first?

Close the main shutoff first. Then call both, since a plumber repairs the pipe and the cleanup crew removes water and dries the structure.

Can I dry the wall myself with fans?

Fans help the surface, but water inside a wall cavity needs measured drying and often an opening. A moisture meter shows what the surface hides.

Who pays for the pipe repair?

That depends on the policy and the cause. The plumber's repair and the drying are often billed separately, so ask your insurer how each is handled.

Is the water from a supply line safe to handle?

Water from a clean supply line is usually treated as clean at first, but it picks up dirt as it sits. Wear boots and gloves, and keep electrical items out of it.
