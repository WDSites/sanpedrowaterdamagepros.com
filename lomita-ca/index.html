Lomita has its own city rules, and the calls reflect it

Lomita is an incorporated city, not a neighborhood. It was incorporated on June 30, 1964, covers about 1.9 square miles and had 20,921 residents at the 2020 census. Because it is its own city, permits, water service and building review come from city offices rather than from the city of Los Angeles.

That matters on the phone. When someone in Lomita calls about a leak, the first questions are who supplies the water, whether the home is rented and which city office handles permits. The sections below answer them.

When water is spreading in Lomita

Emergency calls come from kitchens, bathrooms and water heater closets in small homes and apartments that sit close together. The emergency line is open 24/7. Close the valve at the fixture or the main, switch off power to wet areas only from dry ground and lift belongings off the floor.

Who supplies your water, and who to call at the meter

The city's public works pages list water operations, water quality reports and a water meter shutoff page, which confirms that Lomita runs a water system of its own. California Water Service also serves part of Lomita through its Palos Verdes system. Look at the name on your bill and call that provider about the meter or a street main.

The utility is responsible for its side of the meter. What happens inside the walls is a restoration problem, and the shutoff valve at the house is the fastest control you have.

Rented homes and the Railroad Museum neighborhood

Of Lomita's 8,593 housing units, about 96 percent are occupied and just over 55 percent of those are rented. A leak in a rental calls for the same written notice to the landlord and the same photos as anywhere else. The city's Railroad Museum, opened in 1966, is a familiar landmark for giving directions.

If you are in a unit that shares walls with neighbors, tell them quickly. Water and mold move through walls, and the earlier everyone knows the smaller the repair.

Before we arrive: if water is still spreading

Shut the right valve

Close the fixture valve. If water keeps flowing, close the main at the house or ask the water provider about the meter.

Stay clear of power

Do not touch wet outlets. Use the panel only from dry ground.

Lift and photograph

Raise belongings off wet floors and photograph the source and the damage.

Report it

Tell the manager or owner in writing so the repair can be authorized.

Lomita is served as ZIP code 90717, which covers the whole compact city from the Narbonne Avenue area to its borders with Harbor City and Torrance. If your mailing address uses a neighboring ZIP, call and give your street name.
