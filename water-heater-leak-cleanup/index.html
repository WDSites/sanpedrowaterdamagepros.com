Why water heaters fail and what spills

A tank water heater holds a few dozen gallons at all times. When the steel tank rusts through, a drain valve weeps or a supply connection loosens, that stored water has nowhere to go but out. A failing tank can release most of its contents in one go, then keep dribbling from the supply line.

Most leaks come from a few places. The tank itself corrodes over the years. The temperature and pressure relief valve can discharge if the system is over pressure. Fittings and flex connectors loosen with age. Each source points to a different fix, and a plumber tells you whether the tank needs replacing.

Safety steps before the water spreads

  1. Cut the energy

    Turn an electric heater off at the breaker if you can reach it dry. For a gas heater, turn the gas control to off or pilot.

  2. Close the cold supply

    The cold water valve on top of the tank stops new water from entering. Turn it clockwise.

  3. Do not touch hot water

    Water from a tank can be hot enough to scald. Keep children and pets away from the puddle.

  4. Protect belongings

    Lift boxes, stored items and electronics off the floor and photograph the area.

  5. Call the line

    Say where the heater sits, how much water you see and which rooms it has reached.

California rules and rental calls

California law, Health and Safety Code section 19211, requires water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquake motion, following the California Plumbing Code. A strapped tank can still leak, but a loose one can tear its own connections away in a shake, so check the straps whenever a replacement is done.

Water to San Pedro homes comes from the Los Angeles Department of Water and Power, and the heater sits on your side of the meter. Renters occupy a little over half of Lomita's occupied homes per the Census, so a leaking heater is often the landlord's to replace. Tenants should report a leaking heater to the landlord in writing and keep photos with dates.

Ask the Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety whether replacement work needs a permit before you schedule it.

Drying the area around the heater

Heaters sit in garages, closets, attics and utility rooms, and each location dries differently. A concrete garage floor dries fast. A closet with wood subfloor and drywall holds moisture. The crew checks every surface near the tank with a moisture meter.

Equipment is chosen to match. Wet-vac extractors remove standing water, air movers push air across the floor and wall base, and a dehumidifier pulls moisture from the room. Baseboard and the lower strip of drywall come out if they stay wet. Drying targets are checked against the ANSI/IICRC S500 industry reference, and the fuller cleanup process is explained on its own page.

Once the area is dry, the crew can reset flooring and wall base. See water damage repair for the rebuild step.

Insurance and the heater itself

A sudden discharge from a plumbing appliance is often handled as a covered water event, while damage from slow, long-term seepage is often excluded. The heater itself is typically seen as wear and tear. Read your policy or ask the insurer before you approve work.

Prevention that actually helps

  • Look at the tank base twice a year for rust or moisture.
  • Test the relief valve discharge pipe for drips.
  • Keep stored items off the floor around the heater.
  • Replace a tank that is leaking, rusting or well past its expected life.
  • Know where the cold supply valve is before you need it.

Questions about water heater leaks

Do you respond to a leaking water heater emergency at night?

The line answers all night. For a heater, the crew first asks whether the supply is off and where the tank sits, then brings extraction gear and drying equipment sized for a closet or garage.

Should I turn the heater off before calling?

If you can do so safely, yes. Turn off the power or gas and close the cold water valve, then call.

Does a water heater leak cause mold?

It can if the area stays wet. Drying within a day or two lowers that risk, and the mold page explains what to do if growth has already started.

Who replaces the heater?

A plumber replaces it. The cleanup crew dries the area and can coordinate timing.

Can I keep using hot water if the leak is small?

A plumber should look first. A small leak can turn into a large one, so shutting the heater down is the safer choice.
