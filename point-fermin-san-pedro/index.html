What the 1929 slide teaches about water and slopes

Reference summaries of contemporary reports describe a landslide that began in early 1929 at the east end of Point Fermin Park. The ground was reported to move about 11 inches a day, and a waterlogged layer of bentonite clay was part of the cause. Most houses on the 600 block of Paseo Del Mar were emptied beforehand, and two slid into the sea.

The community plan calls the Sunken City block geologically unsafe, and the area remains fenced. Do not go there to look. The lesson for a homeowner is smaller and practical. Water that soaks into ground on a slope can matter as much as water inside the house, so a leak outside deserves an early look.

If the ground might be moving

New cracks in a retaining wall, a patio that has shifted or a door that suddenly sticks point to a ground question. Drying equipment cannot fix that. Call the Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety about the ground, and call us about the water inside.

First steps and safety before help arrives

Stop the source

Shut the valve at the leaking fixture, or the main valve where the line enters the house, if that is safe to reach. If the main will not turn, LADWP can explain meter shutoff.

Respect the power

Stay out of rooms where water touches outlets, cords or the panel. Do not switch breakers while standing on a wet floor, and call 911 or the utility if you see sparks.

Photograph and lift

Take photos before anything is moved. Lift small items off wet floors and keep pets and children out of wet rooms.

Treat sewage as unsafe

Anything that came up from a toilet or drain is contaminated. Keep people out, leave the household fans off and call before cleaning.

How cleanup works in a Point Fermin home

A crew starts by finding where the water went. Moisture meters and thermal imaging show wet drywall, framing and flooring that look dry. Standing water comes out with extraction equipment, and materials that cannot be saved are removed so they do not hold moisture. Emergency water removal explains this first stage.

Drying comes next, using air movers and low grain refrigerant dehumidifiers. On damp days the outside air cannot help, so the dehumidifiers often carry more of the load than the fans. Readings are taken daily until the structure is dry. See water mitigation for drying and water damage repair for the rebuild.

Industry practice follows ANSI/IICRC S500 as a published reference for this work. That is a standard people can ask about, not a claim about any company.

Insurance and documentation

Coverage depends on the cause of the damage and on the policy. A sudden pipe or appliance failure is treated differently from a slow leak or from flood water, and flood usually needs its own policy. Read your policy or ask your insurer. The California Department of Insurance publishes consumer guidance on claims.

Photos from before anything moved, meter readings and a written scope can help describe what happened and what was done. They do not decide coverage, and no claim outcome is promised here.

Mistakes to avoid after a leak

  • Waiting for a stain to dry on its own, since the wall behind it may stay wet.
  • Running a household fan through sewage or contaminated water.
  • Aiming water toward a slope or the edge of a foundation.
  • Throwing out damaged items before taking photos.

Questions about water damage in Point Fermin

Is emergency water damage help available in Point Fermin?

The number on this page connects you with the line. Describe the wet rooms and how quickly the water is moving, and you will be told what to do first. How soon anyone can come depends on the situation, and this page makes no promise about it.

Does a leak here mean my house is in a flood area?

A plumbing or roof leak is not a flood, so a leak alone says nothing about flood zones. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center to look up a particular address.

Who do I call if the ground or a slope looks like it is moving?

Call the Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety and ask about a geotechnical professional. Water damage crews dry buildings. They cannot stabilize ground.

How many days will drying take?

Typical losses dry in about three to five days, with daily moisture readings deciding the finish. A soaked subfloor or a wall cavity can run longer.

Can the mold question wait until the drying is done?

Damp materials left alone for a couple of days can start to grow mold. If you see or smell mold, say so on the call. Mold work is a separate job, covered on the mold remediation page.
